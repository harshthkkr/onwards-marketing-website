Quick answer: A good vision board example has three things: a small number of specific dreams (five to nine), pictures and words that are personal to you, and a next step for each one. The 12 examples below show what that looks like for a whole year, for one goal, and for students, work, couples and families.

What a good vision board looks like

Most vision board examples online are beautiful collages with no way to tell whether they helped anyone. The ones that work tend to share three traits.

  • It is specific. “Japan in spring” beats “travel more.” A number, a place or a date gives you something to aim at.
  • It is short. Five to nine dreams fit on one board and in one year. Thirty do not.
  • Every dream has a next step. A picture shows where you want to be. The next step is how you start getting there.

Vision board examples by goal

Each example shows nine tiles in a three-by-three grid, with the dream that matters most in the middle. Swap the words for your own pictures.

1. The whole-life board for 2027

The classic. One board for the year that touches every part of your life, built around a single word or theme in the middle.

  • Japan in spring
  • Save $10,000
  • Run a 10K
  • Sunday dinners at home
  • Word of the year: closer
  • Twelve books
  • A reading corner
  • Learn Spanish
  • A weekend with Mum and Dad

Why it works: Each tile comes from a different area of life, so no single goal crowds out the rest.

First step: Pick the tile that scares you most and put a date on it.

2. The travel vision board

For a year when the main thing you want is to go somewhere. One big trip in the middle, smaller ones and the practical steps around it.

  • Cherry blossoms in Kyoto
  • A road trip with no plan
  • The Northern Lights
  • Passport renewed
  • Japan in spring
  • Trip fund: $3,000
  • Learn 20 phrases
  • A solo weekend
  • One new city each season

Why it works: It mixes the dream (the view) with the unglamorous steps (the passport, the fund) that make it real.

First step: Open a separate savings pot and name it after the trip.

3. The career vision board for work

For a promotion, a change of direction or a project you want to lead. Works well pinned beside your desk.

  • The job title I want
  • One course finished
  • A mentor I meet monthly
  • Portfolio updated
  • Lead my own project
  • Ask for the raise
  • Speak at one event
  • Leave on time
  • A desk I like sitting at

Why it works: It names the outcome and the behaviours that lead to it, not just the title.

First step: Book the conversation with your manager this month.

4. The student vision board

For school, college or university. Balances grades with the rest of student life, so the board is about the year, not only exams.

  • The grade I’m aiming for
  • Study abroad
  • A summer internship
  • Asleep by midnight
  • Finish the year proud
  • Join one club or team
  • Read for fun again
  • Save for a laptop
  • A weekend away with friends

Why it works: It is specific about results but still leaves room for friends, rest and fun.

First step: Write the application deadline for the internship in your calendar.

5. The money vision board

For saving, paying off debt or a big purchase. Numbers and dates do the work here, so write them large.

  • $10,000 saved by December
  • Credit card at zero
  • Three months of expenses set aside
  • No-spend weekends
  • The deposit for our home
  • Ask for the raise
  • Side income: $300 a month
  • A weekly money check-in
  • One thing worth the splurge

Why it works: Every tile can be measured, so you always know how close you are.

First step: Set up an automatic transfer for the day after payday.

More vision board examples: home, couples and family

Boards for the place you live and the people you share it with.

6. The health and fitness vision board

For feeling stronger, calmer or more rested. Choose pictures of how you want to feel, not only how you want to look.

  • A 10K in April
  • Eight hours of sleep
  • My first pull-up
  • Cooking four nights a week
  • Feel strong this year
  • A daily walk
  • A swim class I enjoy
  • Phone out of the bedroom
  • Annual check-up booked

Why it works: It pairs one event with a date (the 10K) with small daily habits that support it.

First step: Sign up for the race today, before you feel ready.

7. The home vision board

For a move, a renovation or simply making the place you live feel more like you. Go room by room.

  • A reading corner
  • A herb garden
  • A dinner table for eight
  • A calm bedroom
  • Our own front door
  • Paint the hallway
  • Art on the walls
  • Tidy in ten minutes
  • A neighbourhood we love

Why it works: It includes things you can do this weekend as well as the big long-term dream.

First step: Choose one small corner and finish it completely.

8. The couples vision board

A shared board for two. Each of you dreams separately first, then you put what overlaps in the middle.

  • A weekly date night
  • Japan together
  • Save for the deposit
  • A new recipe every month
  • A home that feels like us
  • Walk the Camino together
  • Phone-free dinners
  • More time with each other’s friends
  • A tradition that’s ours

Why it works: It holds shared dreams and small everyday rituals side by side.

First step: Each pick one tile and plan the very first step together this week.

9. The family vision board

One board everyone in the house adds to, children included. Keep it on the fridge or a shared screen.

  • A summer road trip
  • Game night on Fridays
  • Grandparents every month
  • Each child picks one adventure
  • More time together
  • A garden we grow
  • A family photo on the wall
  • Screen-free Sundays
  • Learn to camp

Why it works: Everyone has at least one tile that is theirs, so everyone cares about the board.

First step: Let the youngest person choose the first thing you do.

10. The one-word theme board

The whole board answers one question: what would this year look like if I were braver? Swap in your own word.

  • Say yes to the talk
  • A solo trip
  • Ask for the raise
  • Sign up for the class
  • Braver
  • Have the hard conversation
  • Publish the thing
  • Swim in the sea
  • Host a dinner

Why it works: A single word makes decisions easier all year, because you can test every choice against it.

First step: Do the smallest brave thing on the board in the next seven days.

Digital vision board examples

11. The phone wallpaper board

A board you see every time you pick up your phone. Use fewer tiles than on paper, because a lock screen is small: six is plenty. Make the image 1080 by 1920 pixels for a phone, or 1920 by 1080 for a desktop background.

  • 10K in April
  • Japan in spring
  • $10,000 saved
  • Closer
  • Twelve books
  • Sunday dinners

Why it works: You see it dozens of times a day without having to remember to look.

First step: Leave space at the top for the clock, so no tile is hidden behind it.

12. The board that turns into a plan

This is a real shared board in Onwards. Each dream is a picture with a title, an area of life and a status: Dreaming, Planning, In Progress or Achieved. Tap a dream and it opens into next steps you can tick off.

Why it works: The board shows what you want and how far along each dream is, so it stays useful after January.

First step: Add one dream and let Plan AI suggest the first few steps.

Vision board layouts that work

The layout matters less than what you put on it, but a clear structure makes a board easier to read at a glance.

  • Grid. Equal tiles in rows, as in the examples above. Tidy, quick to make and easy to turn into a wallpaper.
  • Centre focus. One large image or word in the middle with smaller ones around it. Best when one dream matters more than the rest.
  • By area of life. A section each for travel, home, work, health and people. Best for whole-life boards.
  • By quarter. Four columns, one for each three months of the year. Best when timing matters, as with money and career boards.
  • Collage. Overlapping pictures with no fixed structure. The most expressive and the hardest to act on, so add next steps on the back or in an app.

What to leave off your vision board

  • Thirty goals when you have time for seven
  • Stock photos that could be anyone’s life
  • Other people’s dreams you feel you should want
  • Vague words with no picture of what they mean
  • Anything you would be relieved to drop
  • Dreams with no next step attached

Questions

Good questions, clear answers.

What do you include in a vision board?

Pictures and words for five to nine specific dreams, drawn from the areas of life that matter to you: travel, home, career and money, health, relationships and personal growth. Add a next step to each dream so the board leads to action.

What should I add to my 2027 vision board?

Start with one dream you keep postponing, one health goal with a date, one money number, one thing for your home and one thing to do with someone you love. Then choose a single word for the year and put it in the middle. Our vision board ideas list has 56 more.

What are the main categories for a vision board?

The most common vision board categories are health, career, money, relationships, travel, home and personal growth. You do not need all of them: pick the four to six where you want something to change this year.

How many things should be on a vision board?

Five to nine dreams is a good range. Fewer than five can feel thin, and more than nine is hard to act on in a single year. A phone wallpaper board works best with about six.

Can AI make a vision board?

AI tools can generate images and suggest ideas, but the dreams on the board need to be your own choices. In Onwards, you add the dreams and Plan AI breaks each one into Little Steps you can tick off.
