Quick answer: To make a vision board, choose four to six areas of your life, write one or two specific dreams for each, collect pictures and words that represent them, and arrange them on a board or in an app with the most important dream in the middle. Then add a next step to each dream and keep the board where you will see it every day. Set aside an hour or two.

What is a vision board?

A vision board is a collection of pictures and words that shows what you want your life to look like. It can be a poster on a wall, a pin board, a phone wallpaper or a board in an app. Its job is to keep the things you care about in front of you, so you notice chances to act on them.

What you need to make a vision board

You probably have most of it at home already.

For a paper vision board

  • A poster board, cork board or large sheet of card
  • Printed photos and old magazines
  • Scissors
  • A glue stick, tape or pins
  • Markers or a pen for words and next steps
  • An hour or two without interruptions

For a digital vision board

  • Your phone
  • Photos from your camera roll
  • Saved posts from Instagram, Pinterest or the web
  • An app to arrange them in

How to make a vision board in 7 steps

  1. Look back before you look ahead. Spend ten minutes on the year that is ending. What gave you energy, what drained you, and what do you want more of? Your answers point to what belongs on the board.
  2. Choose four to six areas of life. Travel, home, career and money, health, relationships, personal growth and fun are the usual ones. Picking a few keeps the board about your whole life, not just work.
  3. Write one or two specific dreams for each area. “Get fit” becomes “run a 10K in April.” “Travel more” becomes “Japan in spring.” If you can picture it, it is specific enough.
  4. Collect pictures and words. Use your own photos first, then images saved from Instagram, Pinterest and the web, or cut from magazines. Choose the ones that make you feel something, not the ones that look impressive.
  5. Arrange the board. Put the dream that matters most in the middle and build the others around it. Leave some empty space so the board is easy to read at a glance.
  6. Add a next step to every dream. This is the step most people skip, and it is the one that turns a vision board into a plan. Write the first small action beside each picture: “renew passport,” “book the trial class,” “open a savings pot.”
  7. Put it where you will see it, and check in monthly. A wall by your desk, your phone’s lock screen or an app you open every day. Once a month, tick off what you have done and choose the next step for each dream.

Questions to ask yourself before you start

If step one feels hard, answer these on paper first. The dreams for your board are usually hiding in the answers.

  • What do I keep saying “one day” about? The trip, the class, the move, the conversation.
  • When did I feel most like myself this year? Who were you with and what were you doing?
  • What would I regret not starting? Think a year ahead and look back.
  • What do I want more of in an ordinary week? Small things count: slow mornings, dinners with friends, time outside.
  • What am I ready to leave behind? Making room is part of the plan.

How to choose pictures for your vision board

Personal beats polished. A slightly blurry photo of the street you want to live on will do more for you than a perfect stock image of a house.

  • Your own photos of moments you want more of
  • The actual place: the view, the street, the front door
  • People doing the thing you want to do
  • Numbers and dates written large
  • A word or phrase in your own handwriting
  • Saved posts that keep catching your eye

Paper or digital vision board: which is better?

Neither is better in general. Choose the one you will look at most often.

  • Paper is better for the making. Cutting and arranging by hand is slow in a good way, and a board on the wall is hard to ignore. It is harder to update, and you cannot take it with you.
  • Digital is better for the keeping. It is always in your pocket, easy to change as your plans change, and simple to share with a partner or family. It is easier to forget unless it is on your lock screen or in an app you open daily.
  • Many people do both. Make a paper board in January, take a photo of it, and keep the photo and the next steps on your phone.

Where to put your vision board

  • On the wall beside your desk
  • Inside a wardrobe door you open every morning
  • On the fridge, for a family board
  • As your phone lock screen
  • As your desktop background
  • In an app on your home screen

How to use your vision board after you make it

Making the board takes an afternoon. Using it takes a few minutes a month, and that is where the difference is made.

  • Every day: glance at it. No ritual needed. Seeing it is enough to keep the dreams in mind.
  • Every month: check in. Tick off the steps you have done and write the next one for each dream.
  • Every few months: edit it. Remove what no longer matters and add what does. A board that changes with you is working.

Making a vision board with someone else

A shared board is a good way to start the year with a partner, friends or family. Each person dreams separately first, then you put what overlaps in the middle. Our guide to making a couples vision board walks through it step by step.

Common vision board mistakes

  • Too many dreams for one year
  • Vague goals with no number, place or date
  • Pictures chosen to impress, not because you want them
  • No next step beside each dream
  • Putting the board somewhere you never look
  • Never updating it after January

Questions

Good questions, clear answers.

What materials do you need to make a vision board?

For a paper board: a poster board or cork board, printed photos or magazines, scissors, glue or pins, and a pen. For a digital board you only need your phone, your saved photos and an app to arrange them in.

Which app is best to make a vision board?

It depends on what you want. Design tools such as Canva are good for making a single image to print or use as a wallpaper. Onwards is a free app built for vision boards you act on: you save inspiration from any app, each dream breaks into Little Steps, and you can share a board with a partner, friends or family.

How long does it take to make a vision board?

Set aside an hour or two. About half of that goes on deciding what you want, and the rest on collecting pictures and arranging them. A digital board is quicker if you have already been saving images.

When is the best time to make a vision board?

Late December and early January are the most popular, because you are already reflecting on the year. A birthday, a new job or a move are good moments too. Any time works as long as you come back to it each month.

Do vision boards actually work?

A board will not do the work for you. What helps is being clear about what you want, seeing it often, and attaching a small next step to each dream. That is the difference between wishing and planning.
