Quick answer: A couples bucket list is a shared list of experiences and goals two partners want to do together, from trips and adventures to cosy nights in and big life goals. The best ones mix big dreams with small things you can do this month, and give every item a next step.

How to make a couples bucket list you’ll actually finish

Most couples bucket lists die in a notes app. These five steps are what makes the difference.

  1. Write separately first. Give yourselves ten minutes each, so one person’s ideas don’t crowd out the other’s.
  2. Merge and talk. Read them out. Star anything you both wrote, and be curious about the ones only one of you wrote.
  3. Mix big and small. Aim for a few big dreams, plenty of medium ones, and lots you could do this month.
  4. Give each one a next step. “Go to Japan” becomes “pick a season” and then “set up a savings pot.” Small next steps are what keep a dream moving.
  5. Keep it somewhere you both see. A shared list you open every week beats a beautiful list you never look at.

Travel bucket list ideas for couples

The big ones you talk about over dinner. Pick one, then give it a season and a rough budget so it stops being “someday.”

  • See the cherry blossoms in Japan in spring
  • Take a slow train journey through the Swiss Alps
  • Watch the Northern Lights from a glass cabin
  • Road-trip a coastline with no fixed plan
  • Walk part of the Camino de Santiago together
  • Go on a safari and see the Great Migration
  • Spend a week somewhere neither of you has ever been
  • Stay in an overwater bungalow, just once
  • Visit the town where one of you grew up, with them as guide
  • Island-hop in Greece by ferry
  • Book a last-minute weekend away with only a carry-on

Adventurous things to do together

Shared nerves make great memories. Choose something that scares you both a little.

  • Go skydiving or indoor skydiving
  • Learn to scuba dive and get certified together
  • Hike to a sunrise summit
  • Sleep under the stars on a camping trip
  • Take a hot-air balloon ride
  • Try surfing lessons on the same beach
  • Kayak or paddleboard at sunset
  • Run your first 5K or 10K side by side
  • Do an escape room and beat the clock

Cosy and at-home bucket list ideas

Not every dream needs a passport. These ones fit into an ordinary week, which is why they actually happen.

  • Cook a full meal from a country you want to visit
  • Build a blanket fort and watch a whole film trilogy
  • Start a two-person book club
  • Learn a partner dance in the living room
  • Write letters to open on your next anniversary
  • Recreate your first date
  • Have a no-phones Sunday
  • Grow something together, even if it’s just herbs
  • Make a photo book of your year

Food and drink bucket list ideas

A tasting menu, a market, or the café you keep walking past: food is the easiest shared adventure.

  • Take a cooking class in another country
  • Eat at a restaurant you’ve both saved for months
  • Go on a wine, coffee or tea tasting trip
  • Try every bakery in your neighbourhood and rank them
  • Pick fruit at a farm and make jam with it
  • Host a dinner party for both your friend groups
  • Learn to make fresh pasta from scratch

Learn something new together

Being beginners together keeps things playful.

  • Learn the basics of a language before a trip
  • Take a pottery class and keep what you make
  • Learn to hold a handstand (or any skill you’ve both put off)
  • Volunteer together for a cause you care about
  • Take a photography walk and print your favourites
  • Learn to sail, ski or ride a motorbike

Big life goals for couples

The long-term dreams. Writing them down together is half the work; breaking them into small steps is the other half.

  • Save for and buy your first home
  • Pay off a debt together and celebrate it
  • Plan a career break or sabbatical
  • Adopt a pet
  • Build an emergency fund you both feel calm about
  • Renovate one room, start to finish
  • Plan the wedding, vow renewal or big anniversary trip
  • Live abroad for a month

Seasonal bucket list ideas

Short, seasonal lists are easy to finish and give you something to look forward to every few months.

  • Spring: picnic under blossoms, plant a window box
  • Summer: outdoor cinema, beach day at sunrise, eat outside every night for a week
  • Autumn: pumpkin or apple picking, a cosy cabin weekend
  • Winter: ice skating, a winter market, a hot-chocolate crawl

Questions

Good questions, clear answers.

What should be on a couples bucket list?

A mix of big dreams (travel, a home, a sabbatical), medium adventures (a cooking class abroad, a sunrise hike) and small, cosy things you can do this month. The small ones keep momentum going while you work towards the big ones.

How many items should a couples bucket list have?

There’s no right number, but 20 to 50 works well: enough to choose from, few enough that you still read it. Add new ideas as you go and celebrate the ones you tick off.

What’s the best app for a couples bucket list?

Pick one you will both actually open. Onwards is a free couples app built for this: one shared list, ideas saved from any app, AI that turns dreams into small next steps, and reminders for both of you.

How do we actually complete our bucket list?

Give every item a next step and a rough “when,” review the list together once a month, and celebrate each one you finish. Shared reminders help: it’s easier to follow through when someone is rooting for you.
